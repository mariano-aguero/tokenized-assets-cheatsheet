# Tokenized Assets Cheatsheet

[![Pages](https://img.shields.io/badge/read%20it-mariano--aguero.github.io-a78bfa)](https://mariano-aguero.github.io/tokenized-assets-cheatsheet/)
[![License: MIT](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-MIT-yellow.svg)](LICENSE)

What a tokenized asset actually is, who is allowed to hold it, how a transfer that the token can refuse works, and what happens on the day the chain and the legal register disagree.

**Read it here: [mariano-aguero.github.io/tokenized-assets-cheatsheet](https://mariano-aguero.github.io/tokenized-assets-cheatsheet/)**

The page follows one holding through eight stages:

```
1 · Structure   what is the legal thing, and what does the token entitle you to?
2 · Admission   who is allowed to hold this at all?
3 · Issue       mint against the register, to a verified holder
4 · Hold        who has the keys, and what can they do with them?
5 · Transfer    a transfer that the token itself can refuse
6 · Service     price, yield, corporate actions
7 · Exit        redeem or sell, and where does the cash leg come from?
8 · Repair      freeze, force, recover, when reality disagrees with the ledger
```

## What's inside

- **An interactive walkthrough** at the top: step one operation through eight modules with Back and Forward, or press Play. Six scenarios, from a transfer that is allowed to one the token refuses, an atomic DvP purchase, a lost wallet recovered by the agent, and buying a tokenized share with the dividend that follows
- **Open ended or closed**: why anything gets minted at all when the asset already exists, and why mint and burn is what holds the peg
- **The register question**: is the chain the register or a mirror of it, and the one test that settles it
- **Standards compared**: ERC-20, ERC-1404, the ERC-1400 family in four pieces, ERC-3643, ERC-7518 and the now final ERC-7943 (uRWA), plus asset-shape standards, the non-EVM chains that put the controls in the protocol, cross-chain transport, and the identifiers a tokenized security still needs
- **Identity, not addresses**: on-chain identity, claims and their issuers, compliance modules, the holder cap problem
- **The powers the issuer keeps**: freeze, partial freeze, forced transfer, recovery and pause, and the trade they represent
- **The money leg**: delivery versus payment, why atomic is not the same as T+0, and what happens when the cash leg is off chain
- **Servicing**: NAV and oracles, subscriptions and redemptions, the three ways yield reaches a holder, corporate actions
- **The market as it actually is**: rough sizes per category and who is in each
- **Two companies, two answers**: Securitize owning the whole regulated stack, and Rain tokenizing its own receivables to settle with Visa every day of the week
- **What breaks**: stale NAV, the wrapper disagreeing with the chain, one leg landing, redemption gates, composability that should not compose
- **The patterns, and where they come back**: which one tokenization genuinely removes, which survive unchanged, and the ones the chain itself adds
- **What this shares with payments**, mapped line by line, plus a build order and a field glossary
